# How I work

Four weeks before the new school year, I opened ChatGPT because I wanted to compare two mathematics textbooks. I wanted to use them to build a plan for my class, and I asked GPT: “Can you help me with that?” It did not stop with that one question. The first experiments led to worksheets, prompts, YAML files, learning workshops, libraries, local tools, and web apps.

My work with YAML did not begin because I had a grand plan or because I understood much about it. Quite the opposite: I did not have the slightest idea.

But I was fascinated by the moment when a structure such as a YAML file suddenly made part of my teaching logic visible. It did not stop there: libraries emerged that prevent months of work from disappearing into old chats. I could also recombine scientific models and represent them in didactic matrices.

Anyone who develops with AI knows this feeling. It is the resonance between an unanswered question and an idea lying in the fog, and for me it was curiosity that led me into this unknown field of knowledge. To me, this field combines human judgement with the probabilistic calculations of a language model. Together, the two make it possible for something new to emerge from creativity and knowledge in this process. Something that evokes a feeling when you look at it.

Most of the time, it begins with an initial question, while another question always resonates in the background: Can AI even do this? By now, I know that this question is unnecessary. I build, I develop, I test. I look for new paths, document them, and analyse the solutions and answers I find along the way. In doing so, I often discover another, even bigger question behind them.

And sometimes I ask questions that lead beyond the original project: What would a metacognitive system made up of AI and a human have to look like? Can I leave the chat environments of GPT and Claude and work through a web interface that keeps a locally stored log which GPT and Claude can only read and update?

This GitHub profile shows part of that workshop. Not everything is finished. Not everything worked. But every project has shown me something about how people and AI can work together without the human relinquishing control of the direction.
